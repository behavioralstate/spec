**The person names the connection** ([Agent Registration](../SPEC.md#agent-registration), [Sign-in Guidance](../SPEC.md#sign-in-guidance)). The name they use in the prompt ("sign me in to acme payroll dev") becomes the name the credential is kept under, end to end.

## What was measured

With 0.9.15's words a plain assistant signed the person in correctly, then kept the credential under a name of its own; the credential block's `mcp` entry was keyed by the host, so the person's name for the service reached nothing. And one person has two doors on one platform — a customer surface and a back office, each with its own credential and its own permissions — which need two names they recognise.

## What changes

- **A new device request parameter, `connection`**: one word of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens (`^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,63}$`), made from the name the person gave the service ("acme payroll dev" becomes `acme-payroll-dev`; its host when they gave none). The service keeps it with the registration, unread: it is the consumer's label, never identity. Another shape is `invalid_request` with an `error_description` stating the shape.
- **A new token answer member, `name`**: the `connection` sent, else the address of the sign-in manifest. The credential block's **`mcp` entry is keyed by `name`**; it was keyed by the sign-in manifest's address.
- **The three texts** say it: name the connection first and ask the person to confirm it, send it as `connection`, and keep the credential under the `name` the answer gives back. Another name is another sign-in, kept apart, with its own credential and its own permissions.
- **Name Resolution** binds the credential to that name.

## Every version's words stay valid

`discovery.json` publishes the 0.9.17 texts beside 0.9.14's and 0.9.15's (`$defs/signInGuidance/$defs/0.9.17`), and `authentication.note` accepts any of them. A service carries the texts of the version it declares, or of a later one; a service that declares 0.9.17 carries 0.9.17's words.
