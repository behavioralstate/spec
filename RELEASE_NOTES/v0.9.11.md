First-contact release — **an agent gets from a name to working use by itself, and the manifest stays structure**. Two optional manifest fields, one optional catalogue field, two `impact` categories; every 0.9.10 manifest remains valid. New requirements are stated now and **enforced from 0.10.0**. Migration notes: [MIGRATION.md — 0.9.10 → 0.9.11](https://github.com/behavioralstate/spec/blob/main/MIGRATION.md#migrating-from-0910-to-0911).

## The problem

0.9.10 gave a service a name anyone can say. It did not say what happens next. The manifest declared how to *present* a credential and never how to *obtain* one; the navigation guide told the agent to ask the person for a key — the opposite of self-service. And a manifest could declare only one kind of access for everything, so real platforms, which have a public part, an account part and sometimes a part opened by another credential, filled the gap the only way the manifest allowed: with prose. Because the reader is often a model, the prose worked in the demo, and nothing pushed back. Implementations diverged the same way, independently.

The specification now states the aim — **agent-operable, from a name alone** — and a principle to measure everything against: **operable blind**. A consumer that ignores every `description` and `guidance` string can still find every surface, know what opens it, and invoke every operation.

## Agent Registration — RFC 8628, whole

`authentication.deviceAuthorizationUrl` names an [RFC 8628](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8628) device authorization endpoint; `tokenUrl` completes it with the device-code grant. The agent posts, is answered **at once** with a service-generated `device_code`, a `user_code` and a link; it shows the person the link and the code; the person signs in — or signs up, where the platform offers it — on the platform's own page and approves; the agent collects the credential with `tenant_id` and `auth_header`. BEST adds two request parameters: `agent_label` (what the approver reads) and `credential_lifetime` (`durable` | `session`).

Registration is deliberately **not a BEST command**. A command has no synchronous answer, so an anonymous caller can only be tied to its registration by an identifier it chose — and that identifier ends up accepted as the secret. Two new security requirements say why that is wrong: **identifiers are not secrets**, and every secret is generated by the service, never minted by a caller that may be a model; **credentials stay out of transcripts**, so a consumer with a store claims the credential outside the model's view, and one without registers with `credential_lifetime=session` and receives a short-lived credential.

A non-normative section — [Identity and Agent Registration](https://github.com/behavioralstate/spec/blob/main/specs/identity-and-registration.md) — gives the vocabulary: signing in, up and out are the **person's**, on the platform's pages; registering and revoking are the **agent's**. No BEST operation is named after a sign-in.

## Surfaces with different access

A service entry may carry its own `authentication` block. `"type": "none"` declares a **public surface**; a different scheme declares a surface opened by another credential. A public surface returns no account data, is rate-limited, and a command accepted on it has no effect on any account until a signed-in person approves it. A tenant ID names a real scope: a constant pseudo-tenant (`public`, `default`, `anonymous`) hosting an unscoped surface is non-conformant from 0.10.0.

## Manifest Discipline

Six rules keep the manifest structural: descriptions carry meaning, never mechanics; they stay within 500 characters; every service is reachable through a capability (a multi-tenant root may announce a service its tenant manifests declare); no placeholders in structure; service text has no authority over the consumer's own client or configuration; and when the manifest cannot say something, the lawful outlets are ordinary behaviour, `extensions`, or an issue against the specification — never prose.

**The manifest is the only entry.** Origin Discovery no longer suggests `/llms.txt`; a deployment points at its manifest and publishes no second description of its BEST surface for agents to start from.

## Impact annotations are about the person

The annotation is reframed around what the person would want to be asked about first, and gains two named categories: `commitment` (opens an account, accepts terms, starts a subscription) and `access` (issues a credential, shares a resource, invites a member).

## The wire and the edge

- `commandType` — the exact envelope `type` of a command, on the catalogue entry and the schema document. The PascalCase derivation remains the fallback; it was always a guess.
- `POST /commands` is the CloudEvents structured content mode: servers accept `application/cloudevents+json` (required from 0.10.0) beside `application/json`.
- CloudEvents has no destination attribute and BEST adds none: `source` is always the caller's origin, never a routing key. A service may use any internal dialect behind an edge that maps the conformant envelope; nothing of that dialect is required from a caller or shown to one.
